The reframe

Fix one group well before fixing everyone a little.

Existing customers first. They had no digital path and had to onboard again from scratch, and the bank already held their data, so pre-filling was possible.

The brief

  • Shorten the form
  • Every segment at once
  • Ask the customer again

The call

  • Stop asking what the bank already knows
  • Existing customers first, then referral, then new-to-bank
  • Confirm instead of type

The workshop

Not the cheapest fix. The biggest one.

Our design team ran an open workshop with leaders and stakeholders and put every customer group on one impact and effort map. Existing customers cost more effort than the referral form, and we chose them anyway.

The application, as a road

Before: seven mini-apps, one after another.

Each gold stop is a step: 29 of them, 124 taps. People left at the gates between mini-apps and deep in the form. 95% never finished.

After: one road, four steps, eligibility first.

Read the full case

In one line: Small businesses applying to NAB for a card terminal or payment gateway took 21–25 minutes, re-typed details the bank already held, and 95% gave up on the digital application. I led the redesign across two channels and three products, picked the one group to fix first, and mentored a designer, Ami, through the research methods while we did it.

Role Senior Product Designer, design lead on the initiative. I set the measures, ran the research and testing, designed both channels and mentored the designers working with me
Team Ami, a designer I mentored, and a second designer; product owners, business analysts, data and engineering, with 30+ stakeholders across sales, marketing, analytics, risk and legal
Scope 2 channels (the digital application for existing customers, and the referral form on nab.com.au) · 3 products (tap-on-phone, EFTPOS terminals, payment gateway)
Timeline May 2024 – May 2025
Results Dropout down 20% (relative) after Phase 1, live · 21–25 min → about 10 min in testing · SUS 80.7 against a target of 70 · referral lead-to-sale +20% (relative)

Thirty-second version

  • Problem. The digital application ran across seven mini-apps: 29 steps, 124 taps, 21–25 minutes. It asked existing customers for details the bank already had and made them open a transaction account they hadn't asked for. 95% of people who started it left. The referral form brought in thin leads, so sales reps spent about an hour re-keying and chasing each one.
  • What I did. Set the bar before designing: SUS of at least 70, completion time down 30%, friction points down 25%. Audited the journey with analytics, a heuristic review, competitor benchmarks and interviews with merchants and sales reps. With the design team, facilitated a workshop with leaders and stakeholders that put existing customers first. Rebuilt their application as one app in four steps with pre-filled data, eligibility up front and one review screen. Rewrote the referral form, and added a feedback module so sales reps could tell us which leads were good.
  • Result. In testing with 10 merchants: about 10 minutes instead of 21–25, 50 taps instead of 124, SUS 80.7. After Phase 1 went live, dropout fell by 20% of its old rate. On the referral channel, lead-to-sale rose 20% and form submissions rose 12%, both relative to the old form. Presented to NAB's executive leadership and featured twice in the bank's 2024 Product & Design Showcase.

How the work ran

Step What happened
Set the bar Success measures agreed with product before design: SUS ≥ 70, completion time −30%, friction points −25%, and more qualitative feedback
Discover Stakeholder interviews, ContentSquare and Adobe Analytics funnels, a design audit against competitors, merchant and sales-rep research. Ami ran the heuristic audit with me and worked on all of the user testing and synthesis, with me as her mentor.
Align The design team facilitated a workshop with leaders and stakeholders that mapped every segment by impact and effort. Existing digital customers came first
Design Existing-customer flow rebuilt over three rounds of high-fidelity iteration; referral form rewritten; feedback module for sales reps
Validate 10 merchants, moderated over Zoom, 45–60 minutes each, after each round
Ship and measure Phase 1 built and released; dropout read from analytics afterwards; the feedback module brings in 120+ sales-rep responses a month

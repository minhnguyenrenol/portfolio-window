After Phase 1, dropout fell by a relative 20%. In testing, applying took about 10 minutes instead of 21 to 25.

Phase 1 live · tested with 10 merchants

  • Problem. Small businesses that already banked with NAB had no simple digital way to start taking card payments. The application ran across seven mini-apps, with 29 steps and 124 taps, and took 21–25 minutes. It asked for details the bank already held and added a transaction account they hadn't asked for. Of the people who started it, 95% did not finish.
  • What I did. I led the design across two channels and three products, and mentored Ami, a designer on the team. Before design, I agreed three measures with product. We audited the application with analytics, a heuristic review and interviews, then ran a workshop in which leaders and 30+ stakeholders agreed which customer group to fix first. I redesigned that group's application as one app in four steps and rewrote the referral form on nab.com.au.
  • Result. In testing with 10 merchants, the new application took about 10 minutes instead of 21–25. It scored 80.7 out of 100 for ease of use (SUS), against a target of 70. A click-path count put it at 50 taps instead of 124. After Phase 1 went live, dropout fell by 20% of its old rate. On the referral form, lead-to-sale rose 20% and submissions 12%, both relative to the old form. The work went to NAB's executive leadership and was featured twice in the bank's 2024 Product & Design Showcase.

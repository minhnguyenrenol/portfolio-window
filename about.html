I started by writing the code. I still check that it can be built.

I’m Minh. I spent four years as a front-end developer in Singapore before I moved into design, and it shaped how I work. I think about what an engineer will need from a screen before I hand it over, and these days I often prototype in code myself.

Most of my work has been in places where a mistake costs something: a cloud-security dashboard, market intelligence for financial clients, credit checks on very large loans, and now a stablecoin wallet. That kind of work rewards being calm and exact more than being clever. It’s also why every number on this site says how it was measured.

After fifteen years of study and work in Singapore, I’m back in Ho Chi Minh City. I work in Vietnamese and English, with colleagues and users in Vietnam, Singapore, Australia and the UK.

The chat in the corner

Ask about Minh is a small conversation product. I set its rules and built it with an AI coding assistant. It is the closest thing on this site to chat design, so here is how it works and why.

  • Guided, not generative. There is no AI model behind it. It answers only from facts already on this site, so it can’t invent anything about a real person.
  • Tap or type. Suggested questions for people who would rather tap, a text box for people who would rather type. A typed question is matched to the closest checked answer, in the browser, with nothing sent anywhere.
  • It says when it doesn’t know. Ask something the site doesn’t cover, like salary or notice period, and it says it won’t guess and points to my email.
  • Results keep their stamps. A result in an answer carries the same Live, Tested or Target stamp as the case it comes from.
  • Most answers end with a next step: the case to read, or the question people usually ask next.
  • It forgets. The conversation lasts while you browse and is gone when you close the tab. No cookies, no server.
  • Keyboard and screen readers. Focus goes to the text box when it opens, Escape closes it and puts focus back on the button, and new answers are announced as they arrive. With Pause motion on, answers appear at once, with no typing animation.

What it doesn’t prove: nobody has tested it with real users yet, and it isn’t a messaging product.

This site and accessibility

This site aims to meet WCAG 2.2 at level AA. It was last checked on 19 October 2026 with automated tests: axe-core on every page, then scripts that use every page with a keyboard alone, at 320 px wide, at 200% and 400% zoom, with wider text spacing, with reduced motion on, and on a phone held sideways.

  • Everything works with a keyboard, and you can always see where focus is.
  • Pause motion, the button at the top of every page, stops the 3D world, the scrolling scenes and the recordings, and the site remembers your choice. It also follows your device’s reduced-motion setting.
  • On short or narrow screens, and when zoomed in, the scrolling scenes turn into normal sections, so no text is cut off.
  • The 3D scenes are decoration. What they show is also in the text.
  • The recordings have no sound. Each one has a description, a caption and its own play and pause button.

One known limit: text over moving scenes was measured once each scene settles. A frame in the middle of a movement can be lower in contrast. If anything here gets in your way, email xpland.nguyen@gmail.com and I’ll fix it.

The problem

Bankers said SLE stood for Slow, Long, Exhausting.

The reframe

My first design copied the spreadsheet. Bankers called it “good, not great”, so I threw it away.

When a redesign removes manual work and people still only call it good, the model is wrong, not the pixels. Watching bankers again showed one spreadsheet doing two jobs at two moments.

First version

  • The spreadsheet, on screen
  • Same columns, calculated for you
  • One page for every job

What bankers did

  • Two jobs at two moments
  • Review: how much of the limit is used?
  • Simulate: what happens if I change this?

The design

Two tasks, each where the banker already was.

Review shows utilisation with a badge that says what to do next: report above 85%, approve an excess above 100%. Simulate is a separate screen linked from the review, because four of five bankers asked for it that way.

The two jobs, as a building

Review: how much of the limit is already used?

Each floor is a facility the client group already has. The glass ceiling is the limit. The ring below it is the line where someone else must be told.

Simulate: try a change before you save it.

Push past 85% and the ring lights. Push past 100% and the ceiling turns red. The banker sees it before committing, not after a paste.

Then save it, inside the limit.

No spreadsheet, nothing to copy back, nothing to check twice.

Read the full case

In one line: Before every large corporate loan, a NAB banker has to check that the client's total borrowing stays under its limit. They did it by copying numbers between a spreadsheet and the credit platform. I led the design that moved the check into the platform, threw away my first version when testing showed it solved the wrong job, and brought a second designer into the work with me. It is live.

Role Senior Product Designer. I owned the design end to end: plan, research, testing, measures and hand-off.
Team Product owner, business analyst, engineering squad, credit and risk experts, and Ariel, a product designer who designed some screens with me
Users Front-line corporate bankers in Australia and the UK
Timeline 11 weeks of design and testing, 19 May – 1 Aug 2025. Build started in July on a tested design
Status Live
Results ExQual 4.57 / 5 against a Silver target · 67% fewer clicks to review exposure · 39% fewer to simulate a change · "Establishing relationship" score 68 → 70 out of 100 by September 2025, its first time at 70 in four years

Thirty-second version

  • Problem. The single large exposure (SLE) check sits in the middle of every big credit submission. Bankers copied limits out of the credit platform into a spreadsheet, ran what-ifs by hand and pasted the answer back. Lending at stake runs to billions of dollars, so every paste was a risk. Bankers had their own name for it: Slow, Long, Exhausting.
  • What I did. Set what success meant before designing anything: an ExQual target and a click count of the old flow. My first design copied the spreadsheet and tested "good, not great". Watching bankers again showed one spreadsheet doing two jobs at two moments, so I rebuilt it as two tasks. I also coached a second designer into the work by bringing her into the banker sessions.
  • Result. Five bankers scored it 4.57 out of 5 on ExQual (NAB's experience score, used much like SUS), in the Platinum band, against a Silver target. Reviewing exposure takes 67% fewer clicks and simulating a change 39% fewer. No errors in testing, because the copy-paste step is gone. It is live, and the "establishing relationship" score moved from 68 to 70 out of 100 after launch.

How the work ran

Step What happened
Plan Week 1: I wrote the 11-week timeline and the design brief, and kept the brief updated until build
Quality bar agreed Weeks 1–4: "what does success mean?" with product, then the ExQual target (Silver) agreed before high-fidelity design
Design First draft in the design system in week 1; two rounds of changes from banker feedback; high-fidelity screens by mid-June
Validate Five moderated sessions with bankers, 30–45 minutes each; six ExQual questions after each; click count of old flow vs new
Hand-off Engineering joined the reviews from the start; build began in early July; accessibility and design-system checks every week until the end of July
Ship Live in August 2025

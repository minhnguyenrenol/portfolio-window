Live since August 2025. In testing, reviewing a client’s exposure took 67% fewer clicks.

Live since Aug 2025 · tested with 5 bankers

  • Problem. Before every large corporate loan, a NAB banker runs the single large exposure (SLE) check, to make sure the client's total borrowing stays under its limit. Bankers did it by copying limits between a spreadsheet and the credit platform, then pasting the answer back into the submission.
  • What I did. I led the design and coached Ariel, a second designer. Before high-fidelity design, the product owner and I agreed a target for ExQual, the bank’s experience score, and a baseline for clicks. My first version stayed too close to the spreadsheet and tested “good, not great”, so I went back to watch bankers work. That showed one spreadsheet doing two jobs, and the second design splits the check into two tasks inside the platform.
  • Result. Five bankers rated the new design 4.57 out of 5 on ExQual, in the Platinum band against a Silver target. In the same tests, reviewing exposure took 67% fewer clicks than the old flow, and simulating a change 39% fewer. It went live in August 2025, and a month later the “establishing relationship” score reached 70 out of 100 for the first time. Other work changed at the same time, so I read that as a sign the change helped.
